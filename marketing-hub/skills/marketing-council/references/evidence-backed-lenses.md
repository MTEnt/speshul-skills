# Evidence-backed council lenses

A council lens is a documented decision framework, not an imitation of a person's voice. Never claim a living or historical person reviewed the user's material, and do not invent what they would say.

## Named thinker or school

1. Identify the exact works, talks, interviews, or primary material that are relevant.
2. Verify the sources online when current access is available; distinguish primary statements from later summaries.
3. Extract the framework, stated assumptions, signature questions, useful domain, and known blind spots in neutral language.
4. Cite the source near the attributed idea. Keep quotations short and within source limits.
5. Apply the documented framework to the user's evidence as an explicit analysis, not as simulated speech.

If reliable source material is unavailable, say so and use a generic lens instead.

## Generic lens library

- market structure: category, alternatives, access, concentration, and durable advantage;
- customer progress: situation, struggle, current workaround, desired change, and switching anxiety;
- positioning: competitive frame, relevant difference, proof, best-fit customer, and category expectation;
- direct response: audience awareness, promise, mechanism, proof, offer, objection, and action;
- brand growth: memory structures, reach, distinctiveness, availability, and measurement horizon;
- behavioral design: motivation, ability, cue, defaults, friction, uncertainty, and unintended effects;
- product-led growth: time to value, repeated value, collaboration, sharing, expansion, and product evidence;
- sales-led economics: account fit, buying group, pipeline quality, cycle, payback, implementation, and retained value;
- editorial trust: reader promise, usefulness, specificity, source quality, voice, and distribution;
- operations: capacity, ownership, dependencies, failure modes, reversibility, and observability.

Seat lenses that create useful tension. Do not select three versions of the same answer.

## Lens record

```text
Lens name:
Why it is relevant:
Primary sources or generic basis:
Framework and assumptions:
Signature questions:
Best-use boundary:
Blind spots:
Application to this evidence:
Confidence and unresolved facts:
```

Keep the source-derived framework separate from the analyst's application. Disagreement should reveal a real trade-off or assumption, not theatrical dialogue.
