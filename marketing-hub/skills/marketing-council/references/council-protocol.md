# Council protocol

## Session modes

- **Quick challenge:** three lenses test a bounded decision.
- **Full council:** four or five lenses produce independent recommendations and rebuttals.
- **Red team:** skeptical lenses search for unsupported assumptions, customer harm, economic failure, and operational fragility.
- **Decision review:** assess an existing plan against evidence and stated objectives.
- **Custom lens:** apply a user-specified documented framework with explicit source boundaries.

## Lens bench

Select from:

- category and positioning;
- customer research and jobs-to-be-done;
- demand state and direct response;
- brand memory and distinctive assets;
- behavioral economics and choice design;
- offer, pricing, and unit economics;
- product-led growth and activation;
- sales, channel, and buying-group behavior;
- measurement and causal skepticism;
- customer advocacy, accessibility, and trust;
- operations, legal, and delivery risk;
- competitive strategy and alternative response.

A lens may be grounded in published work by named thinkers when sources are identified, quotations remain limited, and the output is labeled as an application of documented ideas—not simulated speech.

## Protocol

1. Frame the decision and define what is out of scope.
2. Build one evidence packet with sources, dates, assumptions, and unknowns.
3. Seat lenses whose premises can genuinely conflict.
4. Collect independent first positions before exposing other responses.
5. Run rebuttal: strongest disagreement, missing evidence, and decision risk.
6. Perform a current research pass only where facts can resolve disagreement.
7. Chair the synthesis: agreements, conflicts, assumption dependencies, reversible tests, and the decision owner's choice.


## Output

```markdown
# Council review

## Decision and evidence packet
## Seated lenses and why
## Independent recommendations
## Rebuttals and unresolved disagreement
## Missing or newly verified facts
## Chair's synthesis
## Recommended decision and sacrifice
## Test, owner, and disconfirming signal
```

Reject fake quotations, invented biographies, automatic consensus, prestige as evidence, and unnecessary councils for simple production work.
