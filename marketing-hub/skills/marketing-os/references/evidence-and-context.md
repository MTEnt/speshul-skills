# Evidence and context

Use this module when the work depends on product truth, external research, performance data, customer language, or claims that could be mistaken for verified facts.

## Build the factual foundation

Inspect available sources before asking for information again:

- product documentation, application behavior, pricing, policies, and release notes;
- brand, positioning, sales, support, and research documents;
- analytics, CRM, experiment, campaign, and revenue data;
- customer interviews, surveys, reviews, calls, tickets, and win/loss notes;
- current public competitor and category sources.

Use this compact context model:

| Field | What belongs here |
| --- | --- |
| Business outcome | The commercial or operational result being pursued |
| Product truth | What exists, for whom, how it works, price, limits, availability |
| Audience | Buyer, user, situation, trigger, alternatives, disqualifiers |
| Position | Category, differentiated capabilities, value, proof |
| Offer | Deliverable, commitment, price, terms, risk handling, exclusions |
| Voice | Recognizable language, banned language, tone boundaries |
| Proof | Demonstrations, data, cases, reviews, credentials, guarantees actually authorized |
| Channels | Current distribution, audience access, constraints, costs |
| Baseline | Funnel volumes, rates, revenue, time window, data quality |
| Guardrails | Legal, brand, platform, rights, approval, budget, timing |

Do not create a permanent context file unless the user requests it or repeated work demonstrably needs one. When one already exists, update the authoritative file instead of creating a competing copy.

## Label evidence

Use these states internally and expose them when the distinction matters:

- **Verified:** directly supported by a current source, inspected artifact, or reliable dataset.
- **User-provided:** asserted by the user but not independently checked.
- **Analysis:** a conclusion derived from stated evidence.
- **Hypothesis:** a testable explanation or proposed causal relationship.
- **Unknown:** needed but not currently available.

Record source, date or period, scope, and relevant limitation. A quote without speaker, source, and context is not useful evidence. A dashboard number without definition and time window is not a stable metric.

## Research without laundering guesses

- Start from the decision the research must change.
- Prefer primary sources for product, policy, pricing, platform, and legal facts.
- Use customer language as qualitative evidence; do not claim prevalence from a handful of comments.
- Separate direct competitors, substitutes, internal workarounds, and doing nothing.
- Record counterevidence. Do not collect only material that supports the preferred narrative.
- When access is blocked or a source is stale, mark the affected conclusion unverified.
- Quote sparingly and preserve the original meaning.

## Claim ledger

For claim-sensitive work, maintain a small ledger:

| Proposed claim | Status | Evidence | Allowed wording | Owner or next check |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Product capability | Verified / user-provided / unknown | Source or observation | Exact, hedged, or omit | Who can confirm |
| Outcome or metric | Verified / user-provided / unknown | Dataset and period | Exact, qualified, or omit | Measurement owner |
| Customer proof | Approved / unapproved / unknown | Case, quote, permission | Named, anonymous, or omit | Approval owner |

If support is absent, remove the claim or clearly frame it as a goal, hypothesis, or illustrative scenario.

## Adversarial check

Before relying on the foundation, ask:

- What fact would most damage this strategy if false?
- Is the loudest customer segment actually the buyer or only the easiest to observe?
- Does the baseline mix incompatible definitions, cohorts, or time windows?
- Is a competitor claim their positioning language or demonstrated product behavior?
- Is the requested channel solving the real constraint, or merely the most visible activity?
