# SEO audit system

## Technical access

Inspect DNS and HTTPS, host consistency, redirects, status codes, broken resources, robots rules, XML sitemaps, URL discovery, canonical signals, duplicate variants, index directives, pagination, parameters, filters, faceted navigation, mobile behavior, essential rendered content and links, performance, interaction stability, structured data, and migrations.

Compare submitted, discovered, crawlable, renderable, canonical, indexed, ranking, and converting URL sets where data exists. Do not infer indexation from a crawler or source HTML alone.

## International and localized sites

Check language and region strategy, distinct URLs, hreflang values and reciprocity, canonical interaction, locale defaults, redirects, translated navigation and metadata, local availability and currency, duplicate content, geolocation assumptions, and search-console targeting where currently supported. Verify current official requirements.

## On-page review

For each representative template inspect audience intent, page job, unique useful content, title, primary heading, description, visible labels, media alternatives, product and entity clarity, internal links, anchor meaning, evidence, freshness, next action, and source-to-page continuity.

Avoid keyword density targets and rewriting text merely to repeat phrases.

## Content quality

Look for original evidence, experience, useful calculation, clear sourcing, ownership, correction path, information gain, appropriate depth, and fit to query intent. Flag thin, duplicated, contradictory, stale, mass-generated, or inaccessible material.

Do not claim to detect AI authorship from style. Review observable quality and provenance problems instead.

## Site-specific checks

- ecommerce: category and filter behavior, product availability, variants, reviews, merchant data, and discontinued products;
- SaaS: product, feature, integration, comparison, pricing, documentation, and use-case relationships;
- local: entity consistency, service area, location pages, hours, reviews, and local structured data;
- publisher: taxonomy, archives, authorship, news or article markup, pagination, and update handling;
- marketplace: listing quality, duplicate inventory, seller or provider entities, and internal discovery;
- JavaScript application: server response, render path, route discovery, metadata, link semantics, and error states.
