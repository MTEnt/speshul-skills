# Ad-creative system

## Grounded inputs

Extract audience situation and tension, desired progress, current alternative, product mechanism, demonstration, proof and claim limits, offer and commitment, objections, existing source assets, rights, brand rules, landing promise, and qualified-performance evidence.

## Concept model

```text
Concept ID:
Audience and situation:
Problem or desire frame:
Point of view and promise:
Mechanism or demonstration:
Proof:
Offer and CTA:
Visual device and format:
Why this differs from the control:
```

Concept families include problem recognition, outcome, mechanism, product demonstration, comparison, objection, proof, founder or operator, creator experience with valid disclosure, editorial explanation, customer story with permission, graphic data, and offer framing.

## Hook system

A hook can open with recognizable situation, contradiction, useful result, visible demonstration, costly mistake, unanswered question, proof, comparison, or specific promise. It must remain truthful after context appears. Avoid bait that attracts an irrelevant audience or creates a message mismatch.

## Format taxonomy

- static product, editorial, comparison, diagram, testimonial with permission, or offer card;
- carousel or document with one decision per frame;
- short-form demo, founder explanation, creator-led experience, screen recording, before-and-after only with verified evidence, motion typography, or narrative scene;
- chat, note, search, notification, or interface-inspired device only when it is clearly an ad and does not impersonate a private communication;
- longer explainer, webinar clip, case evidence, or retargeting answer;
- responsive search text assets with current field validation.

Choose format from communication job, evidence, placement, production capacity, and audience behavior—not trend alone.

## Platform and production spec

For each asset record platform, campaign, placement, current dimensions and duration, concept ID, audience, opening frame or line, scene or layout sequence, on-screen text, voice or captions, proof source, CTA, destination, source assets, rights, disclosure, accessibility, editable master, and export name.

For generative media, track tool or model, date, prompt, references, identifiers, edits, rights, and disclosure. Never prompt for a living artist's identity or fake documentary proof.

## Video brief

Make the premise clear without sound, identify the intended situation early, show the product truthfully, use captions, preserve reading time, and end with a proportionate action. Specify beat, visual, spoken line, text, duration, transition purpose, sound, source, and rights.

## Static template logic

Use one hierarchy: attention cue, core idea, product or evidence, brand recognition, and action. Check final mobile size, contrast, crop, safe zones, logo use, text amount, and placement policy.

## Batch generation

Create a matrix across a small set of concepts, formats, and audience or proof dimensions. Assign stable IDs. Do not produce a Cartesian explosion of weak variants. Validate character counts, duplicates, claims, URLs, assets, and naming deterministically when possible.

## Performance iteration

Analyze delivery, spend, qualified response, downstream conversion, retention or sales quality, frequency, fatigue evidence, and creative attributes at the concept level. Preserve attribution limits. Generate new work from supported mechanisms while keeping a control and changing interpretable dimensions.

## Creative review page and roadmap

A review surface includes campaign context, concept rationale, every asset at representative size, copy, source and rights, destination, reviewer decision, requested change, and version. The roadmap tracks concept, hypothesis, format, audience, production status, launch window, result, learning, reuse, and retirement.

## Output

Return research summary, concept territories, selected hypotheses, complete copy, production briefs, asset matrix, review presentation, rights state, test plan, QA, and learning record.
