# Behavioral model library

## Think before persuading

Use inversion, second-order effects, opportunity cost, bottleneck analysis, base rates, feedback loops, and system boundaries to test whether the proposed intervention solves the real problem.

## Attention and comprehension

Relevant concepts include salience, selective attention, processing fluency, information scent, chunking, progressive disclosure, serial-position effects, distinctiveness, and cognitive load. Improve hierarchy and meaning before adding stimulation.

## Motivation and progress

Examine goal relevance, expected value, self-efficacy, progress visibility, endowed progress, completion tension, autonomy, identity consistency, and effort justification. Do not turn motivational framing into pressure detached from real value.

## Risk and uncertainty

Consider loss sensitivity, ambiguity aversion, status-quo preference, omission bias, regret, switching cost, trust, reversibility, guarantees, trials, and proof. Distinguish perceived risk from actual product or contractual risk.

## Choice and comparison

Use anchoring, reference points, framing, defaults, choice architecture, decoys, compromise effects, partitioning, and attribute evaluability only with truthful options and transparent consequences. A default must be appropriate and easy to change.

## Social information

Social proof, authority, similarity, reciprocity, commitment, norms, and word of mouth can reduce uncertainty. Verify source, representativeness, incentives, and relevance. Never fabricate customers, consensus, popularity, credentials, or endorsements.

## Memory and brand

Review category entry points, retrieval cues, repetition, spacing, consistency, distinctive assets, narrative, peak and ending effects, and availability in memory. Repetition cannot repair an unclear or untrustworthy promise.

## Pricing behavior

Inspect reference price, metric, partitioning, unit framing, left-digit effects, loss framing, annual commitment, free versus trial, fairness, and pain of payment. Show total cost and avoid exploiting confusion.

## Growth and diffusion

Use adoption thresholds, social transmission, network effects, habit loops, feedback, diminishing returns, channel saturation, and cohort effects. Separate a product loop from incentivized spam.
