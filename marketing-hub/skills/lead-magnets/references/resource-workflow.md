# Resource workflow

## Format selection

- checklist for repeatable verification;
- template for a structured artifact;
- workbook for guided reasoning or data collection;
- spreadsheet for calculation or tracking;
- guide for a bounded decision or process;
- report for original evidence and interpretation;
- swipe file for rights-cleared examples with analysis;
- content upgrade for a next step tightly related to a specific source asset;
- resource library for maintained, searchable utility.

Use a free-tool workflow when computation, personalization, transformation, validation, or live data materially improves the result.

## Match the decision stage

Early-stage resources help define a problem or criterion. Evaluation resources compare approaches, estimate cost, or reduce risk. Purchase resources support implementation, stakeholder approval, migration, or planning. Customer resources accelerate setup and use.

Do not gate basic facts needed for an informed purchase.

## Resource contract

Define outcome, sections, instructions, examples, editable or printable format, accessibility, source evidence, rights, version date, update owner, and next reasonable step. Include enough context for standalone use.

## Gating

Choose ungated, preview then gated, or contact-gated based on user value, acquisition economics, sensitivity, and permission. Explain exactly what is collected and sent. Provide delivery even when tracking fails where the commitment was already accepted.

## Landing and delivery

The landing surface states audience, task, resource contents, format, time or effort, proof, data use, and access method. Cover validation, duplicate request, delayed email, expired link, mobile download, assistive technology, and support.

## Promotion

Use relevant search pages, source-content upgrades, product surfaces, social demonstrations, newsletters, partners, sales, events, and paid promotion. Match promise to actual resource depth.

## Measurement

Track qualified views, opt-in, delivery, open or access where reliable, completion, output use, next-step behavior, activation or pipeline, unsubscribe, complaint, production cost, and support burden. Public benchmarks are context only; compare against the resource's own audience and history.

## Output

Return concept and rejected options, finished content, landing and delivery copy, data and rights requirements, distribution, lifecycle follow-up, QA, measure, and refresh or retirement condition.
