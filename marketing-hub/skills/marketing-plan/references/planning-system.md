# Planning system

## Three phases

### Diagnose

Assess this path:

`market access -> qualified attention -> understood value -> credible desire -> action -> activation -> retained value -> referral or expansion -> commercial return`

For every transition, capture volume, rate, quality, cost, time, owner, and data confidence where evidence exists. Also inspect positioning, offer, proof, product readiness, support capacity, channel access, production capacity, lifecycle and sales handoffs, measurement, cash, and decision rights.

### Choose

State the selected audience situation, expensive or urgent problem, current alternative, category or frame, offer, route to market, current constraint, advantage, evidence, and work deliberately excluded.

Compare options using audience access, urgency, differentiation, proof readiness, unit economics, time to signal, operational capacity, reversibility, and measurement quality. Scores are judgment aids; expose the reasoning.

### Operate

Turn the thesis into now, next-if, and later-or-deferred work. Add owner, capacity, dependency, review cadence, decision rights, stop rules, and the artifact each initiative produces.

## AARRR diagnostic

Use acquisition, activation, retention, referral, and revenue as a completeness check, not a mandate for equal work. Define the qualifying behavior and denominator at each stage. Include lead and sales stages where the business is not self-serve.

## Thirteen-section plan

1. Executive decision and the sacrifice it requires.
2. Evidence scope, baseline, confidence, and material unknowns.
3. Business model, customer economics, and operating constraints.
4. Audience, buying group, situation, and bad fit.
5. Positioning, offer, pricing, proof, and claim limits.
6. Current-state journey audit and primary constraint.
7. Strategic thesis and rejected alternatives.
8. Acquisition and distribution roles.
9. Conversion, activation, lifecycle, sales, and retention work.
10. Measurement, attribution, experimentation, and learning agenda.
11. Budget scenarios, people, partners, tools, and decision rights.
12. Risks, dependencies, stop conditions, and recovery.
13. Thirty-, sixty-, and ninety-day execution plus a conditional longer roadmap.

## Budget method

Start with available cash, maximum acceptable loss, sales and fulfillment capacity, expected customer value, payback assumptions, learning volume, production cost, tools, contractors, events, and contingency. Create conservative, base, and expansion scenarios only when the inputs support them.

Do not use arbitrary percentage splits or assume platform-reported return equals incremental profit.

## Growth pattern and stage

Account for lumpy deals, cohort effects, seasonality, long learning cycles, channel saturation, and delayed retention evidence. Match capability to stage:

- founder-led discovery and proof before heavy scale;
- repeatable acquisition and activation before channel proliferation;
- lifecycle, RevOps, and specialization after volume justifies them;
- portfolio and incrementality methods only when data and spend can support them.

Funding stage is context, not a universal prescription.

## Team and partner model

For each function, state whether the accountable owner is founder, generalist, specialist, agency, contractor, or system. Define the decision retained internally, access granted, handoff, review, cost, and exit condition. Do not outsource an unresolved strategy and expect production volume to solve it.

## Client and business variations

Adapt the plan for self-serve SaaS, sales-led B2B, marketplace, local service, ecommerce, agency or professional service, developer product, and pre-launch work. Preserve the common evidence and operating contracts while changing journey stages, buying group, sales cycle, economics, and feasible channels.

## File output

Use one authoritative plan unless separate operating files are needed. Optional companions are an initiative register, metric dictionary, source appendix, budget model, campaign calendar, and decision log. Keep facts linked rather than duplicated.

## Completion test

The near-term plan can be executed by the named team, one constraint and thesis are visible, deferred work is explicit, metrics determine actions, and assumptions remain labeled.
