# Sales-enablement system

## Sales deck

Build a decision sequence: audience context and stakes, current approach, desired progress, differentiated mechanism, product demonstration, proof, fit, implementation, commercial model, risk handling, and agreed next step. Tailor depth to the meeting and role.

## One-pager

Include audience situation, costly problem, offered progress, mechanism, proof, fit and non-fit, implementation or next step, source date, owner, and contact route. Keep it scannable without reducing claims to slogans.

## Objection library

For each objection record exact language and source, underlying risk, qualifying question, truthful answer, proof, when to concede, disqualifying condition, and next step. Separate objection from a real lack of fit.

## Competitive battlecard

Use current sourced facts, customer-led criteria, competitor strengths, audience fit, trade-offs, landmines to avoid, discovery questions, proof, switching considerations, and unknowns. Do not instruct sellers to make unverifiable attacks.

## ROI and value tool

Define inputs, source, ranges, calculation, time horizon, customer and business outcomes, costs, uncertainty, sensitivity, explanation, and misuse boundary. Never present modeled value as guaranteed savings or revenue.

## Demo script

Capture participant roles, prior discovery, desired outcome, environment and data, story, product actions, proof, checkpoints, likely failure, recovery, security or technical questions, and next step. Demonstrate the customer's workflow instead of touring every feature.

## Case brief

Use only approved customer material. Include starting situation, scope, intervention, product role, result, measurement method, time period, confounders, quotation permission, customer review, and source. Do not imply causality beyond evidence.

## Proposal

State client situation, objectives, scope, deliverables, responsibilities, timeline, assumptions, exclusions, price, payment, change control, acceptance, rights, confidentiality, cancellation, validity, and next step. Coordinate legal terms through authorized review.

## Sales playbook and buyer cards

Define target situations, buying roles, discovery, qualification and disqualification, process, messages, proof, demo, objections, competition, commercial policy, handoffs, tools, coaching, and feedback. Buyer cards separate daily user, champion, approver, procurement, security, and executive concerns when roles differ.

## Governance

Every asset needs audience, owner, source evidence, approved claims, reviewed date, editable source, distribution location, feedback channel, and expiry trigger. Remove stale price, logos, security statements, product behavior, and proof.

## Output

Return the finished asset, usage notes, claim and proof sources, necessary role variants, seller guidance, owner, review date, and update condition.
