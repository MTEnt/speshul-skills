# Offer system

## Value diagnosis

Assess the customer's expected useful outcome and confidence in achieving it against delay, effort, sacrifice, financial exposure, implementation risk, and switching risk. Treat this as a qualitative model unless evidence supports numerical inputs.

Strengthen the offer by improving the actual mechanism, proof, scope, support, time to useful progress, customer fit, or risk allocation before adding promotional devices.

## Complete offer anatomy

- qualifying customer and disqualifiers;
- triggering problem and promised progress;
- mechanism and delivery model;
- included product, service, access, artifacts, and support;
- prerequisites and customer responsibilities;
- sequence and time to first useful result where evidenced;
- price, payment, renewal, cancellation, refund, and tax treatment;
- proof available at the buying moment;
- risk allocation and authorized guarantee;
- exclusions, capacity, and support boundaries;
- next step and what happens after commitment.

## Offer formats

Choose according to delivery and buying risk: fixed-scope service, productized service, implementation package, audit or diagnostic, subscription, cohort, membership, licensing, usage bundle, paid pilot, proof-of-concept, retainer, performance component, or hybrid. Define handoff, change requests, overages, success conditions, and exit.

For self-serve software, packaging and value metric often matter more than a direct-response value stack. Route price-level and tier decisions to pricing work.

## Bonuses

A bonus is justified only when it removes a real obstacle, accelerates use, reduces implementation work, or supplies a missing companion asset. State the obstacle, actual deliverable, rights, support cost, and whether it remains useful without an inflated assigned price.

## Guarantee and risk allocation

Choose among no guarantee with clear trial or proof, satisfaction window, service correction, milestone-based commitment, conditional outcome, performance credit, or refund. Define eligibility, customer obligations, request method, evidence, time window, exclusions, abuse handling, accounting, and operational owner.

Never guarantee a result controlled by customer behavior, third parties, markets, or unverified assumptions without terms that truthfully allocate that risk.

## Scarcity and urgency

Use only real capacity, enrollment, inventory, scheduling, expiry, regulatory, event, or price-change constraints. State what ends, when, why, who is affected, and what remains available. Remove the message when the condition ends.

## Diagnostic loop

1. Locate the failed buying decision and evidence.
2. Classify the cause and plausible alternatives.
3. Change one meaningful offer dimension.
4. Verify delivery, policy, billing, support, and claim readiness.
5. Test or roll out with quality, refund, margin, and retention guardrails.
6. Keep, revise, or retire based on customer and economic outcomes.

## Deliverable

Return the diagnosis, complete offer, message hierarchy, rejected choices, proof requirements, economics and operational risks, policy dependencies, implementation assets, and decision rule.
