# Comparison pages

## Formats

- **Our product versus one competitor:** direct evaluation for a defined audience.
- **One competitor versus another:** neutral category education where our product may be an additional option.
- **Alternatives to one product:** several approaches for a known switching or exploration intent.
- **Best options for a situation:** criteria-led shortlist based on a specific job.
- **Migration page:** switching sequence, data, risk, compatibility, and support.

## Research

Use current product pages, documentation, pricing, terms, changelogs, demos, status, and verified hands-on evidence. Use reviews and communities as attributed experience reports. Record geography, plan, version, checked date, and source.

Separate claimed capability, observed capability, inferred fit, and unknown behavior.

## Content architecture

1. Clarify audience and decision.
2. Summarize who should consider each option.
3. Explain criteria and why they matter.
4. Compare workflow, capability, service, price, implementation, and constraints.
5. Show evidence and meaningful unknowns.
6. Explain better-fit cases and trade-offs.
7. Provide switching, trial, demo, or evaluation next steps.

Use tables for exact repeated fields; use prose for nuanced trade-offs. Avoid dozens of shallow yes/no rows.

## Search and page system

Map page to real query intent, canonical URL, unique title and opening, internal links, structured data only when valid, update owner, and consolidation rules. Do not create near-duplicate permutations of the same comparison.

## Output

Return source and criteria table, message hierarchy, complete page copy, comparison component specification, metadata, internal links, claim caveats, update owner, and conversion path.
