# Research modes

## Analyze existing material

Inventory interviews, sales calls, surveys, tickets, chat, churn notes, reviews, product behavior, and prior studies. Build a codebook around triggers, desired progress, alternatives, pain and cost, urgency, objections, decision roles, switching forces, experienced outcomes, and exact language.

Record each observation with source and context. Treat synthesized or AI-generated summaries as leads until checked against raw material.

## Research public signal

Use current reviews, forums, search results, public communities, social posts, competitor discussions, job language, and procurement material only where access and terms permit. Track URL, date, community or author context when appropriate, product or alternative, duplication risk, incentives, and authenticity concerns.

Choose sources based on the actual audience. A highly visible community is not automatically representative.

## Interviews

Recruit against explicit qualifying criteria. Ask about a real recent event rather than hypothetical preference:

1. What changed or triggered action?
2. What happened next, step by step?
3. Which alternatives were seriously considered?
4. What made the issue costly or urgent?
5. What nearly stopped the decision?
6. Who else shaped it?
7. What happened after adoption or rejection?
8. How is success judged now?

Avoid pitching, leading language, stacked questions, and asking participants to design the solution.

## Surveys

Give each question one analytical purpose. Use open responses for discovery, carefully defined choices for measurement, neutral ordering, clear time frames, and an explicit eligible population. Pilot wording and response logic before distribution.

Do not interpret convenience samples as market estimates. Report response count, denominator when known, recruitment, exclusions, missing data, and likely bias.

## Synthesis

1. Normalize sources without erasing segment or chronology.
2. Code observations consistently and record counterexamples.
3. Cluster by situation, cause, desired progress, alternative, objection, and outcome.
4. Rank themes by decision relevance, evidence strength, and frequency only where defensible.
5. Tie each implication to the observations that support it.

## Persona and segment contract

Create a persona only when a distinct situation or role changes product, message, channel, or buying behavior. Include qualifying conditions, desired progress, alternatives, decision criteria, objections, reachable contexts, sourced language, evidence strength, and exclusions. Do not add fictional names, stock-photo identities, hobbies, or unsupported demographics.

## Deliverables

Return the decision and scope, source inventory, method, findings with supporting and conflicting evidence, material segments, customer-language library, implications, limitations, open questions, and next research step. For primary work, also provide recruitment criteria, consent handling, interview or survey instrument, analysis plan, and owner.
