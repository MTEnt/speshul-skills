# Lifecycle sequence blueprints

These are job maps, not mandatory message counts. Remove any message that lacks a distinct recipient need. Timing must follow the product's normal decision or usage cycle.

## Subscriber welcome

1. Deliver the promised resource or confirm the subscription; set sender and frequency expectations.
2. Help the reader make one useful decision connected to why they subscribed.
3. Show the relevant point of view, method, or product mechanism with evidence.
4. Offer the next proportionate action and stop promotional follow-up after conversion.

## Trial or account activation

1. Confirm access and direct the user to the shortest credible value path.
2. Detect the first missing milestone and offer contextual help.
3. Demonstrate a useful outcome or workflow, not a tour of every feature.
4. Address the highest-evidence obstacle for the user's state.
5. Explain the real trial or access boundary and available choices without fake urgency.

Branch by completed behavior. A user who already reached the milestone must not receive instructions that assume they failed.

## Evaluation and nurture

1. Name the costly or frustrating situation using verified audience language.
2. Teach a criterion for judging alternatives.
3. Explain the mechanism and its limits.
4. Present valid proof for the relevant segment.
5. Resolve a material objection or implementation concern.
6. Invite a fitting next step; suppress on sales acceptance, disqualification, or conversion.

## Launch or release adoption

Use announcement, relevance by segment, demonstration, proof or use case, and a final reminder only when a real deadline or event exists. Existing customers, inactive users, prospects, and press contacts usually require different messages.

## Renewal and expansion

Surface realized value, current usage, renewal terms, decision date, administrative action, and support route. Expansion should follow an observed unmet need or capacity constraint, not simply the calendar.

## Inactivity, payment failure, cancellation, and win-back

- Inactivity: reflect the last reliable state, offer the smallest recovery action, and provide support or preference choices.
- Payment failure: state the account impact and safe update route; do not expose sensitive billing data.
- Cancellation: confirm consequences, export or retention behavior, effective date, and reversible options without obstruction.
- Win-back: use the stated cancellation reason, meaningful product change, or changed circumstance. Stop after a bounded attempt.

## Message specification

For every message return:

```text
Message ID and job:
Eligibility and trigger:
Delay rationale:
Subject and preview:
Body:
Primary CTA and destination:
Fallback fields:
Exclusions and stop state:
Required service or legal content:
Primary measure and guardrail:
```

The final sequence must also include a state diagram, collision priorities, global frequency rule, authentication and deliverability prerequisites, plain-text behavior, and activation approval.
