# Video system

## Choose the approach

- product capture for real workflow or output;
- live action for human credibility, environment, or physical behavior;
- programmatic rendering for repeatable data, typography, UI, localization, or variants;
- motion design for controlled explanation and hierarchy;
- generative video for non-documentary visual worlds or bounded shots;
- avatar when consistent presenter delivery is needed and synthetic identity is disclosed;
- stock and edit for contextual footage with valid rights;
- hybrid when methods can be combined without obscuring truth.

## Beat and edit anatomy

1. immediate premise or recognizable situation;
2. tension, question, or desired progress;
3. mechanism, demonstration, or evidence;
4. payoff and limitation where relevant;
5. next action proportionate to intent.

For each beat specify visual, spoken line, on-screen text, duration, transition purpose, sound, source, rights, and continuity constraints.

Analyze pacing, shot scale, camera movement, composition, typography, transition, color, sound, narrative, and production method when reverse-engineering a reference. Recreate principles, not footage, branding, distinctive sequence, or an artist's identity.

## Product-demo workflow

Prepare stable environment, clean and realistic data, account and privacy state, cursor and focus behavior, zoom or crop, screen size, script checkpoints, likely failure, recovery, and truthful final state. Record at sufficient resolution and frame rate for the intended crop.

## Programmatic video

Define data schema, templates, fonts and licenses, asset identifiers, render environment, deterministic inputs, duration calculation, safe zones, localization, queue, concurrency, retries only for safe transient failures, partial output, naming, and batch validation. Keep rendering reproducible.

## Generative video

Specify subject, action, shot size, camera, motion, environment, lighting, duration, continuity, physics, aspect, reference, negative constraints, and transition handles. Generate bounded shots and edit selectively rather than expecting one long prompt to produce a finished narrative.

Track model, date, prompt, reference, seed or job ID, output, selected take, extension or interpolation, edits, and rights. Verify current provider terms and identity restrictions.

## Avatars and synthetic presenters

Require identity consent or an authorized synthetic identity, script approval, pronunciation, disclosure, gesture and gaze expectations, localization review, voice rights, and removal process. Never imitate a real person without authorization.

## Agent-native pipeline

Use a sequence of brief -> sources -> script -> storyboard -> asset manifest -> generation or capture -> edit -> caption -> encode -> QA -> approval. Each automated step records inputs, outputs, identifiers, failure, and human decision. External publication remains a separate authorized action.

## Accessibility and delivery

Provide accurate captions, transcript, readable text, audio description or descriptive context where needed, flashing and motion limits, loudness and music balance, poster, metadata, and reduced-motion alternative for interactive surfaces.

Verify duration, aspect, resolution, frame rate, codec, bitrate, color, audio, captions, file size, crop, thumbnail, and playback against current platform requirements.

## Output

Return concept and method, script, beat sheet or storyboard, shot and asset list, prompts or code specification, edit notes, rights and disclosure, captions and transcript, export matrix, editable source or reproduction record, and QA.
